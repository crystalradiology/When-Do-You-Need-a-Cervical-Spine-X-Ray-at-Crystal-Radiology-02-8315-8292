A Cervical spine X-ray at Crystal Radiology is a diagnostic imaging examination that produces pictures of the bones in your neck. It helps healthcare professionals assess the alignment and structure of the cervical vertebrae and identify certain abnormalities.
In this article, we explain what a cervical spine X-ray involves, when it may be recommended, how the examination is performed, and what conditions it can help identify.
What Is a Cervical Spine X-Ray?
The cervical spine is the upper section of your backbone, located in your neck. It consists of seven vertebrae, labelled C1 to C7, which support your head and help protect the spinal cord.
A cervical spine X-ray uses a small amount of ionising radiation to produce images of these bones. The images allow radiologists to examine the alignment, spacing, and structure of the cervical vertebrae.
Your doctor may recommend this examination to investigate:
•	Persistent or unexplained neck pain.
•	Neck stiffness or restricted movement.
•	Suspected fractures following an injury.
•	Possible vertebral misalignment.
•	Degenerative changes affecting the neck joints.
•	Certain abnormalities involving the cervical vertebrae.
A Cervical spine X-ray at Crystal Radiology can provide useful information to help your referring doctor understand the condition of your neck bones and decide whether further investigation is required.
When Might You Need a Cervical Spine X-Ray?
Neck pain can develop for several reasons, including muscle strain, injuries, age-related changes, and problems affecting the joints or spinal structures.
Your healthcare professional may recommend a cervical spine X-ray depending on your symptoms, medical history, and physical examination.
Common reasons include:
1. Persistent Neck Pain
Ongoing neck discomfort may require further assessment, particularly when it does not improve with initial treatment. An X-ray can help identify certain bone and joint abnormalities that may contribute to the symptoms.
2. Neck Injuries and Trauma
Following an accident, fall, or sports injury, an X-ray may be used to investigate suspected damage to the cervical vertebrae.
However, CT is often preferred when a significant traumatic cervical spine injury is suspected because it provides more detailed images of the bones.
3. Numbness or Tingling in the Arms
Some people experience tingling, numbness, or weakness that travels from the neck into the shoulders, arms, or hands.
These symptoms may be associated with irritation or compression of nerves. An X-ray can assess bone alignment and degenerative changes, although MRI is generally more useful for examining spinal nerves, discs, and other soft tissues.
4. Reduced Neck Movement
If you find it difficult to turn your head or move your neck comfortably, your doctor may recommend imaging to assess the cervical vertebrae and joints.
5. Suspected Degenerative Changes
Age-related wear and tear can affect the joints and discs in the cervical spine. X-rays may show changes such as reduced disc space, bone spurs, and other signs of cervical spondylosis.
What Conditions Can a Cervical Spine X-Ray Help Identify?
A cervical spine X-ray can help assess several abnormalities involving the bones of the neck.
1. Fractures: X-rays may identify certain breaks in the cervical vertebrae, although CT may be required for a more detailed assessment.
2. Vertebral misalignment: The images can show whether the cervical vertebrae appear properly aligned or whether there is an abnormal position between bones.
3. Dislocation: Certain joint alignment abnormalities may be visible on X-ray and require further medical assessment.
4. Cervical spondylosis: This is a common age-related condition involving changes to the bones and joints of the neck. X-rays may show bone spurs and narrowing of the spaces between vertebrae.
5. Structural abnormalities: The examination may help identify certain congenital or acquired changes affecting the cervical spine.
It is important to remember that an X-ray mainly shows bones. It cannot reliably assess every cause of neck pain, particularly problems involving muscles, nerves, ligaments, or intervertebral discs.
Are Cervical Spine X-Rays Safe?
X-rays use ionising radiation to produce diagnostic images. The radiation dose from a standard cervical spine X-ray is generally low, and the examination is performed when medically appropriate.
The radiographer follows established radiation safety procedures to obtain the necessary images while keeping exposure as low as reasonably achievable.
If you are pregnant or may be pregnant, tell your healthcare professional and radiographer before the examination so they can assess the appropriate imaging approach.
